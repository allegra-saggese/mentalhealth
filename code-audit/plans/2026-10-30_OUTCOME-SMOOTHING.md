# Are the Poor MH Days and Frequent Mental Distress series survey information or model output? (2026-10-30)
Internal note (data provenance). Code: `script4b-diagnostics.py --only outcome-smoothing` (files `Data/output/tables/2026-10-30_4b-outcome-smoothing_*`). Panel 2026-10-25. Nothing here is a finding about CAFOs.

## What County Health Rankings says (pages read 2026-10-30)
- [Poor Mental Health Days](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/quality-of-life/mental-health/poor-mental-health-days) and [Frequent Mental Distress](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/quality-of-life/mental-health/frequent-mental-distress): since the 2016 release the county estimates are single-year CDC multilevel-model estimates (for Frequent Mental Distress "combined with county-level poverty and contextual effects"); since the 2021 release CDC's PLACES uses multilevel regression and poststratification linking BRFSS to the American Community Survey; "the smaller the population or sample size of a county, the more the estimates are derived from the model itself".
- Before the 2016 release, Poor MH Days used up to seven years of landline-only BRFSS responses.
- CHR: the estimates "may not be comparable across years" or across states, and it advises against tracking progress with these measures.
- Not stated on the pages: the PLACES model specification (priors, covariates); whether the 2016-2020 method used covariates beyond county poverty. Both would need CDC's PLACES methodology.
So both outcomes, not only Frequent Mental Distress, are model output, with method changes in the 2016 and 2021 releases.

## What the panel shows (mean over years unless stated)
1. **Spatial reach (Moran's I by distance band, 0-50 / 50-100 / 100-200 / 200-300 / 300-500 km):** Frequent Mental Distress 0.80 / 0.72 / 0.61 / 0.52 / 0.42; Poor MH Days 0.66 / 0.60 / 0.52 / 0.45 / 0.36; unemployment (BLS, direct) 0.72 / 0.60 / 0.49 / 0.38 / 0.30; deaths of despair 0.28 / 0.25 / 0.18 / 0.16 / 0.10. Both mental-health series are correlated further out than the direct comparators, but unemployment is a real regional gradient too, so this test alone does not separate model smoothing from geography.
2. **Dependence on the control set (cross-section R-squared by year):** Poor MH Days 0.27-0.33 in 2010-2015, **0.49-0.60 from 2016** (0.40 in 2023); Frequent Mental Distress 0.65-0.67 in 2017-2022 (0.53 in 2023); unemployment 0.21-0.47; deaths of despair 0.18-0.29. The jump at 2016 matches the change to the model-based method.
3. **Shrinkage by county size (sd across counties, smallest / largest population quartile):** Poor MH Days 0.90 / 0.61, Frequent Mental Distress 2,320 / 1,639, unemployment 2,658 / 1,960, deaths of despair 11.3 / 5.5. Small counties are more dispersed for every series, including the directly measured ones; there is no sign that small counties are pulled to a common value.
4. **Breaks and carried-forward values:** the sd of the one-year change in Poor MH Days is 0.89 in 2016 against 0.19-0.42 in other years; for Frequent Mental Distress it rises from 521-597 (2017-2020) to 895 / 755 / 1,145 in 2021 / 2022 / 2023. **Every county's value in 2015 (Poor MH Days) and in 2019 (both) equals the previous year's exactly**; 14-15% of Poor MH Days values are unchanged in 2012-2014. Within-county persistence (deviation from the window mean on last year's): Poor MH Days 0.87 (2010-2015), 0.81 (2016-2020), 0.98 (2021-2023); Frequent Mental Distress 0.71 (2016-2020), 0.995 (2021-2023); unemployment 0.70; deaths of despair 0.15. The cause of the duplicated years (the CHR release cycle, or the lag mapping in `script0c`) was not traced.
5. **Mechanical channel (TWFE of the large-dairy treatments on the model's inputs, county and year effects, state-clustered):** child poverty per 100k rises with T3 (+152, p = 0.0005) and T4 (+343, p = 0.037), T1 (+222, p = 0.063), E2 (+230, p = 0.10); median household income rises with every treatment (+315 to +846, p <= 0.025); unemployment falls (p 0.06 to 0.32). The effects are small in size, but the treatments move inputs that sit in both the control set and, presumably, in CDC's model.

## What it implies (for the owner to weigh)
- The series are smooth, spatially correlated and strongly tied to socio-economic inputs, and they change method in 2016 (Poor MH Days) and 2021 (both). Spatial dependence in the outcome (rho 0.46 and 0.83 in the Durbin runs) is therefore partly a property of the measure.
- The 2016 break falls inside the Poor MH Days estimation window (2010-2023) and inside the pre-period of the 2017 cohort; year effects absorb only a common shift.
- The treatments move model inputs (test 5): part of a measured effect on the modelled outcome can come through the inputs without a change in mental health. The control set already contains child poverty, median income and unemployment (some are treated as mediators in the register).
- Options, not chosen: restrict to the 2021-onward method years (Frequent Mental Distress only has 2016-2023); drop the carried-forward years; use a direct-survey or administrative outcome; model the break explicitly. Each changes the estimation window or the outcome and belongs to the owner.
